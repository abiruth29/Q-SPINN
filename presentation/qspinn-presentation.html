Slide 2

Introduction: Market mechanics

1. The Stock

A stock represents a fractional ownership (a "share") in a company. When you buy a stock, you own a piece of that business.

2. The Market

The Stock Market (like NSE) is the central venue where buyers and sellers meet to exchange these shares for capital.

3. Limit Order Book (LOB)

A real-time electronic ledger tracking all pending buy and sell orders at various prices. The exchange engine matches trades here.

The Regime Shift Problem
Sudden changes in market behaviour invalidate learned patterns.

The Instability Challenge: Financial markets are highly non-stationary. A standard model trained during a quiet period will catastrophically fail when a macroeconomic event triggers a sudden "regime shift," drastically altering volatility and asset correlations.

Our Objective: We need a structural anchor to keep predictions stable and grounded even when empirical data patterns suddenly collapse.

Slide 5

Research Gap

Gap 1: Static Physics

Classical models (Black-Scholes, Heston) assume constant volatility and log-normal distributions — real markets have fat tails, jumps, and regime breaks.

Gap 2: Brute-Force DNNs

Deep learning processes every tick identically — spending full compute on every tick, including uninformative ones. No mechanism to distinguish signal from noise. Overfits to regime-specific patterns.

Gap 3: Synchronous Correlations

Standard covariance matrices assume simultaneous movement. Real assets show time-lagged correlations (e.g., AAPL drops → MSFT follows 200ms later) — invisible to real-valued methods.

No existing architecture combines event-driven processing, physics-informed learning, and complex-valued correlation modelling in a single framework.

Slide 6

Problem Statement

Design and validate a hybrid neural architecture (Q-SPINN) that integrates spiking neural network gating, quantum-inspired complex-valued covariance modelling, and physics-informed loss constraints to achieve regime-aware, event-driven prediction of limit order book mid-price direction — outperforming existing methods while reducing redundant computation by >90%.

✓ In scope

3-asset LOB basket (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA). FI-2010 + Binance data. Classical GPU training.

✗ Out of scope

Real quantum hardware. Live trading deployment. Multi-asset portfolios beyond 3.

Slide 7

Objectives

O1

Build an SNN gating mechanism that filters >90% of non-informative LOB ticks before they reach the prediction engine.

Metric: Spike ratio, computational FLOP reduction vs. always-on baseline.

O2

Implement a complex-valued covariance layer that captures both correlation strength (amplitude) and time-lag (phase) between assets.

Metric: Phase accuracy vs. known lead-lag pairs; ablation vs. real-valued baseline.

O3

Embed a modified Black-Scholes PDE as a physics-informed loss constraint (PINN layer) to regularize predictions during regime shifts.

Metric: PDE residual loss; prediction stability during volatility spikes.

O4

Validate on FI-2010 + Binance LOB datasets using walk-forward backtesting and Deflated Sharpe Ratio.

Metric: DSR, F1-score for mid-price direction, comparison vs. LSTM/Transformer baselines.
